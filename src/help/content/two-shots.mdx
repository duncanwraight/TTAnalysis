For each point you enter who won, the winning shot and the losing shot. Nothing else from the rally. This page explains why, and what that means for the numbers.

## Why not every shot

A rally in club table tennis is often six or eight shots, sometimes many more. Entering every one would take far longer than the match itself, and most people would give up after a match or two. Two shots a point keeps a match to about 15 minutes at 1.5x speed, and it captures the part of the rally that decided it.

## What the two shots are

- **The winning shot** is the last shot of the player who won the point.
- **The losing shot** is the last shot of the player who lost it.

The losing shot is one of two things. Either it set up the winner (they push, you loop past them), or it missed (you loop, they miss the block). Both are worth knowing: a push that keeps getting attacked is as much a problem as a loop that keeps going long.

## What the data can tell you

- Which shots win and lose points, for each player, on each wing.
- Which pairs keep coming up, such as a backhand push followed by a forehand loop winner. See [Patterns](/help/analysis-page#patterns).
- How the serve and the receive went. When the serve is the winning shot, the receive missed. When the serve is the server's losing shot, the receive won. These are the only two shots of a rally the data places exactly. See [Serve and receive](/help/analysis-page#serve-and-receive).
- When points were won and lost: by stage of the set, from 8-all, and in runs.

## What it can't tell you

- **Shots in the middle of a rally.** If a point went loop, block, loop, block, smash, only the last block and the smash are entered. The counts never say "you played 40 forehand loops", only "the forehand loop won 12 points and lost 9".
- **Where the ball went, how much spin it had, or which serve it was.** None of that is entered. The analysis and the coach's summary never guess at it. Instead they tell you what to look for on the video.
- **Who attacked first**, unless you tick it. The last two shots only show an attack when it came at the end. That's why attacked first is a [sweep](/help/unforced-errors-and-attacks) of its own.
- **Whether a miss was forced**, unless you tick it. That's what the unforced error tick is for.

## What this means when you read it

Treat the analysis as a map of where to look on the video, not a verdict. A shot that lost a lot of points could be a weak stroke, the wrong ball to play it on, or a strong shot from the opponent before it. The numbers tell you which shot to watch, and the video tells you why.
