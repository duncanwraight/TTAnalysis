The coach's summary is a written review of one match, from an AI model (Claude, made by Anthropic). It reads the same points as the analysis page and writes up the problems that cost points, what to try, and the mental side when the numbers show it. It sits on its own tab next to the analysis.

It's switched on account by account, by invitation, while it's being tried out. Anyone can read a summary on a share link.

## What it reads

- every point of the match, with both shots, the hand and any ticks,
- the analysis built from them, the same numbers as the analysis page,
- a guide to each shot used: what's worth checking about it, such as placement, length or spin, and notes on the technique,
- both players' styles, hands and rubbers, if they were set,
- the player's notes, if they have any.

It sees one match at a time. It doesn't compare with the player's other matches.

## What it writes

- **A headline:** one or two sentences on how the match was decided, with the result in sets and the points won.
- **Work on:** three or four problems, each with what the counts show and advice in two parts.
- **The mental side:** up to two notes, only for something that happened more than once, each with something practical to try. For example, losing sets from a lead, slow starts, long runs of points to the opponent, or unforced errors bunching late in sets.

Nerves only come up in that last case: 3 or more unforced errors from 8-all, at least twice as often per point as in the rest of the match. The note then suggests committing to the shot: choosing it early and playing it fully.

## Tactical and technical advice

Each problem's advice comes in up to two panels:

- **Tactical:** something the player can change during a match, such as where they place the ball, how they vary it or which shot they choose.
- **Technical:** something in the stroke itself, or in reading spin, to work on in practice.

Some problems only get one:

- **Unforced errors** get technical advice only. The player went for the right shot and missed it, so there's nothing tactical to change, and the advice never blames misreading spin, because an unforced error by definition wasn't a misread.
- **Receive problems** get advice on reading the serve rather than on the stroke used to return it. A missed receive is nearly always a misread serve.
- **A situation**, such as losing the ends of sets, gets no technical advice.

For example, on a backhand loop that kept losing points, the tactical panel might suggest playing it to the crossover rather than wide, and the technical panel might suggest checking on the video whether the bat angle is closing too early against backspin.

## The rules it follows

- **Only the counts are stated as fact.** "Your backhand loop won 5 points and lost 12" is fact. Why it lost is not: the data doesn't hold placement, spin or which serve it was, so the summary never guesses. It names what to check on the video instead.
- **Styles are taken as given, never as evidence.** The advice never tells a player to stop playing their own style.
- **Notes shape what it checks, and are never quoted.** The summary never says that notes exist or who wrote them. A note comes through as a neutral observation, followed by what the match showed, such as "you can sometimes be too passive, but in this match you attacked first on 14 of 20 points".
- **Who attacked first is used when it's been ticked.** Unconfirmed ticks are treated as "in the points you marked", and [unclear](/help/glossary#unclear) points are never counted as the opponent's.

## Correct a summary

If the summary gets something wrong, choose **Correct this summary**, write what it should change, and choose **Rewrite with my notes**. Corrections of fact are treated as true. For example: "most of my lost backhand loops were against heavy backspin pushes, not his serve."

Your notes are kept and used every time the summary is rewritten. They're never shown on a share link. **Change your notes** edits them, and clearing them rewrites the summary without them.

## When it's out of date

A summary shows as out of date when something it read has changed: a point was edited or ticked, a shot's guide changed, a style or a player's note changed, or the coach's instructions were updated. The tab gets a dot and the summary shows **Update the summary**. Only the owner sees either: a share link and a PDF show the summary as it is.

Writing a summary takes up to a minute.

## Check it against the video

It only knows the last two shots of each point, like the rest of the app. Treat what it suggests as a list of things to look for on the video, not a verdict.
